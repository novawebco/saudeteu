O que é o curry em pó?

Curry powder is a delicious mix of spices, perfect balance to give food just the right kick. Many cuisines have adopted this mix, and each recipe uses its variations. It is often presumed to be a powdered form of the curry leaf, but this is not the case. The curry leaf is a separate herb also associated with the same region as curry powder. Curry powder is a staple ingredient in Pakistani, Indian, Bangladeshi and even South African dishes. It, however, has become an internationally acclaimed spice mix so much so that recently England has chosen curry as its national dish. Many cultures and spices are entwined in this flavorful mix. The roasted dry spices all have their signature flavors combined in this mix. This use of multiple spices blended to add flavor to a dish first originated in Pakistan and India, and curry powder is assumed to be of Eastern origin. A food researcher states that the word curry comes from the Tamil word Kari which means spiced sauce. It was adopted originally in Europe as a thin dressing for vegetable or meat. There are recipe books from the 17th century in Portuguese that have a recipe for the curry powder. The Pakistani and Indian cuisine have many staple dishes that require a must addition of the curry powder. The name curry powder is a western invention, and the first English recipe using curry powder was published in 1747. The curry powder is a westernized version of the spice mix known as garam masala that is a part of almost all Pakistani and Indian dishes. Curry powder is mostly ground by women themselves in the Eastern world, but as the world of food progresses it is now available packaged and ready to use. The composition and mix may vary from brand to brand. It houses a blend of spices such as nutmeg, cinnamon, turmeric and much more. Theses spices on their own are packed full of health benefits.
